Most small business sites lose the customer before the page loads.

A hungry person looking for dinner gives a restaurant's website a few seconds. If the menu is a slow PDF, the hours are buried and the page jumps around on a phone, they go back to the search results and pick someone else. The same is true for a plumber, a clinic or a shop.

I build sites that pass that test. The important things are on the first screen. The page loads fast on a weak signal. It reads well on a phone, because that is where most customers are.

How Codex fits in.

Codex is OpenAI's agent for building software. You describe what you want in plain English and it writes and changes the code. That removes most of the slow, repetitive part of making a website.

It does not remove the need for judgment. I decide what the site should say and how it should look, direct the agent, read what it produces and test the result on real phones. The outcome is a hand-finished site in less time, and changes later are quick: a new menu, a new page or a holiday notice does not turn into a project.
