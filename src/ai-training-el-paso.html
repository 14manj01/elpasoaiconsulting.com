Most AI training is a slideshow. This is practice.

Your company may already pay for ChatGPT or Copilot. The usual result is that two people use it every day and everyone else tried it once. The tool is not the problem. Nobody showed your team how it applies to their own job.

I taught university lab courses at Baylor for four years, and the lesson that stuck is simple: people learn by doing the work, not by watching someone else do it. So my workshops use your documents, your emails and your real tasks. People leave having already done something useful with AI.

What your team learns.

How to ask

Writing prompts that get a usable answer the first time, and how to fix one that misses.

Everyday uses

Email, proposals, summaries, meeting notes, spreadsheets, research and translation between English and Spanish.

When not to trust it

How AI makes things up, how to check its work, and which tasks still need a person.

Privacy and safe use

What is safe to paste into an AI tool, what is not, and which settings to change on day one.

Role-specific skills

Separate exercises for the front office, sales, marketing, operations and management.

A simple AI policy

A one-page set of rules for your business, so everyone knows what is allowed.
