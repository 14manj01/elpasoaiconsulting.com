Nobody opens a business to do paperwork.

Invoices arrive by email, on paper and as photos from a delivery driver. Someone types them into the books, usually late at night. On the other side, customers who owe you money get reminded when somebody remembers.

This is the kind of work AI is now good at. It can read an invoice in almost any form, pull out the vendor, date, items and total, and put them where they belong. It can notice that a price went up. It can see that an invoice is two weeks late and write the reminder. What it should not do is decide to pay anything, and with my setups it never does.

What gets handled.

Supplier invoices, entered

Bills from email, PDFs and photos become draft entries in your accounting software.

Price jumps, flagged

When an item costs more than it did last time, you hear about it before it eats your margin.

Customer invoices, sent

Finished jobs and events become invoices from your notes, ready for you to review.

Late payers, reminded

Overdue invoices are listed each week with a reminder drafted for every one.

Mismatches, caught

Invoices are checked against orders and statements, and the ones that do not agree are set aside.

The month, summarized

A short month-end note: what came in, what went out, and what looks unusual.

Two ways to run it.

I recommend one based on how many invoices you handle.

An AI agent

Claude Duet or a ChatGPT dot, connected to your email and QuickBooks. It checks in on a schedule, drafts the work and waits for your approval. Best for owners who do their own books. AI agent setup

A built automation

A dedicated workflow that watches an inbox all day and processes every invoice the same way. Best for higher volume or a shared accounts inbox. Workflow automation

You stay in charge of the money.

  • AI never pays. It prepares, checks and reminds. Payment stays with a person.
  • Nothing is sent without a yes. Reminders to customers wait for your approval until you decide otherwise.
  • Uncertain means flagged. A smudged total or an unknown vendor goes to you, not into the books.
  • There is a record. You can see what was entered, when, and from which document.
