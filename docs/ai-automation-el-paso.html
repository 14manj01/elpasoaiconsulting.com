Learn to automate the repetitive work.

Give the repetitive work to software.

Every business has work nobody was hired to do: copying details from an email into a spreadsheet, retyping an invoice, chasing a customer for a signature, building the same report every Friday. It is necessary, it is dull, and it is where mistakes creep in.

Older automation needed everything to arrive in a fixed format. AI changes that. It can read a messy email, a scanned form or a photo of a receipt, work out what it says, and pass the right details to the next step. That means far more of your day can be automated than a few years ago.

I teach you to find those tasks, build the automation yourself and test it against your real documents until it holds up.

What you learn to automate.

Email and inboxes

Sort incoming mail, draft replies for approval, and route requests to the right person.

Documents and data entry

Pull names, dates and amounts from invoices, forms and PDFs straight into your systems.

Scheduling and follow-up

Book appointments, send reminders, and follow up on quotes that have gone quiet.

Quotes and invoices

Turn job notes into a formatted estimate, and turn finished jobs into invoices.

Reports

Weekly numbers gathered from several sources and delivered as a summary you can read in a minute.

Border and freight paperwork

Shipment emails, status updates and repetitive forms for logistics and customs brokerage teams.

How the training runs.

  1. Pick one task

    We choose a task that is frequent, annoying and safe to automate, so the first win comes quickly.

  2. Map it

    Together we write down each step as your team does it today, including the exceptions nobody mentions.

  3. Build and test

    You build the automation in your own account with me beside you, and we run it on real examples until the results are right.

  4. Run it yourself

    Your team knows how it works, how to pause it and what to do when something looks wrong. I do not manage it afterward.

People stay in charge.

I do not teach setups that send money or message customers with nobody watching. Each automation has a clear owner on your team, a log of what it did, and an approval step wherever a mistake would be costly. If your data is sensitive, the AI part can run on your own computers instead of someone else's cloud.

When a job needs more than connecting existing apps, you can learn to build your own AI tools to fill the gap.

Questions, answered.

What business tasks can AI automate?

Good candidates are tasks that repeat, follow a pattern and involve text or documents: sorting and answering email, pulling numbers out of invoices and forms, updating spreadsheets or a CRM, sending reminders, drafting quotes and building weekly reports.

Do I have to replace my current software?

Usually not. Most automation connects the tools you already have, such as Gmail or Outlook, QuickBooks, Google Sheets, Excel and your scheduling or CRM software.

What happens when the AI gets something wrong?

AI makes mistakes, so you learn to design for it. Anything that sends money, contacts a customer or changes a record can wait for a person to approve it, and every automation keeps a log you can check.

How long does it take to learn?

It depends on the task. A single well-defined task can often be automated and tested within days; connecting several systems takes longer. You get a plan and a quote for the training before we start.

Which automation tools do you teach?

Whatever fits the job and your budget: Zapier, Make, n8n or Microsoft Power Automate. You own the account, so the automation keeps running without me.
