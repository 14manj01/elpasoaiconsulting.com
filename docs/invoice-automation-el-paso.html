Invoice automation

Invoices that handle themselves.

Supplier bills read and entered. Price jumps flagged. Late-paying customers reminded. You approve with a tap instead of losing a night to paperwork.

Book a free consultationAll services

Nobody opens a business to do paperwork.

Invoices arrive by email, on paper and as photos from a delivery driver. Someone types them into the books, usually late at night. On the other side, customers who owe you money get reminded when somebody remembers.

This is the kind of work AI is now good at. It can read an invoice in almost any form, pull out the vendor, date, items and total, and put them where they belong. It can notice that a price went up. It can see that an invoice is two weeks late and write the reminder. What it should not do is decide to pay anything, and with my setups it never does.

What gets handled.

Supplier invoices, entered

Bills from email, PDFs and photos become draft entries in your accounting software.

Price jumps, flagged

When an item costs more than it did last time, you hear about it before it eats your margin.

Customer invoices, sent

Finished jobs and events become invoices from your notes, ready for you to review.

Late payers, reminded

Overdue invoices are listed each week with a reminder drafted for every one.

Mismatches, caught

Invoices are checked against orders and statements, and the ones that do not agree are set aside.

The month, summarized

A short month-end note: what came in, what went out, and what looks unusual.

Two ways to run it.

I recommend one based on how many invoices you handle.

An AI agent

Claude Duet or a ChatGPT dot, connected to your email and QuickBooks. It checks in on a schedule, drafts the work and waits for your approval. Best for owners who do their own books. AI agent setup

A built automation

A dedicated workflow that watches an inbox all day and processes every invoice the same way. Best for higher volume or a shared accounts inbox. Workflow automation

You stay in charge of the money.

  • AI never pays. It prepares, checks and reminds. Payment stays with a person.
  • Nothing is sent without a yes. Reminders to customers wait for your approval until you decide otherwise.
  • Uncertain means flagged. A smudged total or an unknown vendor goes to you, not into the books.
  • There is a record. You can see what was entered, when, and from which document.

Examples.

Illustrative setups that show what is possible. They are not client results.

A restaurant's produce bill

The produce invoice arrives as a photo. AI enters each line in the books and notes that tomatoes cost more than last month. The owner sees it in the morning brief.

A catering invoice gone quiet

Two weeks after an event, the invoice is still unpaid. The agent drafts a polite reminder with the invoice attached, and the owner approves it from a phone.

A contractor's supplier statement

The monthly statement from a supply house is checked against the invoices on file. Two that do not match are flagged before anything is paid.

Questions, answered.

Can AI really manage my invoices?

It can manage the routine work: reading invoices from email, photos and PDFs, entering them as draft bills, matching them to orders, listing what is overdue and drafting reminders. A person still approves payments and handles disputes.

Does it work with QuickBooks?

Yes. AI agents such as Claude Duet connect to QuickBooks directly. Stripe and most accounting software that accepts imports can work too. I check yours in the free consultation.

Will the AI pay bills on its own?

No. I do not set up AI to move money. It prepares, checks and reminds. Paying a bill stays with you or your bookkeeper.

What about paper or photographed invoices?

Current AI reads photos and scans well, including many handwritten notes. Anything it is unsure about is flagged for a person instead of guessed.

Do I still need a bookkeeper or accountant?

Yes. This takes the data entry and the chasing off their plate and yours. It does not replace professional judgment, tax advice or a review of the books, and I am not an accountant.

How long before it is working?

It depends on your volume and software. A single task, such as weekly overdue reminders, is usually the first thing running. You get a timeline with the quote.
