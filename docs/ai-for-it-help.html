AI for IT help

Your first IT call can be to AI.

Most small-office tech problems can be solved by the person standing next to them, if they know what to ask. I teach you and your staff to use AI to troubleshoot, set things up and stay secure.

Book a free consultationAll training

Small businesses do not have an IT department.

They have whoever is least busy. So the Wi-Fi password is on a sticky note, the router is the one the internet company left behind, nobody is sure when the last backup ran, and a new computer sits in its box for a month because setting it up is a chore.

An AI assistant changes what that person can do. Describe the problem in plain words, add a photo of the error, and it walks you through the fix one step at a time. It will not replace an IT professional for the big jobs. It does stop the small ones from piling up.

What you learn to do.

Troubleshoot step by step

Slow Wi-Fi, a printer that went offline, a computer that crawls. How to describe the problem so AI can narrow it down.

Read error messages

Photograph the screen or the blinking light and get a plain-English explanation of what it means.

Set things up

A new computer, email on a phone, a shared calendar. AI writes the steps for your exact device.

Plan your backups

What needs backing up, where it should go, and how to run a test restore so you know it works.

Tighten security

Two-step sign-in, a password manager and updates, set up by you with AI explaining each choice.

Know when to stop

The signs that a problem is beyond a do-it-yourself fix and needs a professional.

How the training works.

We use the problems you already have.

  1. Bring a real problem

    The Wi-Fi that drops, the email that will not sync, the backup nobody set up.

  2. Solve it with AI

    You work the problem with an AI assistant while I show you what to ask and how to check the answer.

  3. Learn the safe habits

    What never to paste into a chat, when to ask for an explanation and when to stop.

  4. Handle the next one

    The next time something breaks, you know how to start.

Why IT and AI belong together.

Every AI tool sits on top of ordinary IT. An AI agent that reads your inbox depends on your email accounts being set up correctly. AI phone answering depends on your phone service and internet. Private AI depends on a computer and a network you can trust.

When someone on your team understands both, AI projects stop stalling on small things like a forgotten admin password. The security questions also get asked before the AI is switched on, not after.

What I do not do.

I do not manage your network, monitor your computers, hold your passwords or answer support calls. Your systems stay yours. If you need someone on call, a managed IT provider is the right choice, and this training will help you ask them better questions.

Questions, answered.

Do you provide IT support?

No. I do not manage networks, computers or accounts, and I am not a help desk. I teach you to use AI to solve everyday problems yourself and to know when to call a professional.

Can AI really fix computer problems?

It can guide you through most everyday ones. It explains error messages, suggests what to check and in what order, and writes step-by-step instructions for your exact device. It cannot touch your equipment and it can be wrong, so I teach you how to check its advice.

Is it safe to follow AI's instructions?

Usually, with care. I teach three habits: ask it to explain a step before you do it, never paste a password into a chat, and stop before any step that deletes data or resets a device.

When should I call an IT professional instead?

When money, customer data or the whole business is at risk: a suspected hack, a failed drive or server, card-payment equipment, or anything you still do not understand after AI has explained it twice.

Is my business too small to worry about security?

No. Small businesses are common targets because they are easy ones. A few basics, such as two-step sign-in, a password manager, updates and tested backups, stop most of what goes wrong.

Who is this training for?

Owners, office managers and whoever has ended up as the computer person. No technical background is needed.
