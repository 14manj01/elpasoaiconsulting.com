AI for hiring and scheduling

Resumes sorted. Shifts filled.

A stack of applications and next week's schedule are two jobs most owners do late at night. I teach you to hand the sorting and the first draft to AI, and keep the decisions for yourself.

Book a free consultationAll training

Two jobs that land on the owner.

You post an opening and get forty applications. Reading them properly takes an evening you do not have, so the stack sits for a week and the best people take other jobs. Most applicants never hear back at all.

The schedule is the other one. Every week someone fits availability, time-off requests and the busy nights together by hand, and then a call-out on Saturday undoes it.

AI is good at both kinds of reading and sorting. It is not good at judging people, and it should not try. So the setup I teach is simple: AI prepares, and a person decides.

What you learn to do.

Write the job posting

A clear posting from a few notes, in English and Spanish, with the requirements that matter stated plainly.

Sort the applications

AI summarizes each resume against your requirements, so you read short summaries first and full resumes only where it counts.

Prepare the interview

Questions drawn from each application, and one scoring sheet so every candidate is asked the same things.

Reply to every applicant

Drafted replies for the people you are calling and the people you are not, sent after you approve them.

Build the weekly schedule

A first draft from availability, time-off requests and the staff each shift needs, with conflicts and overtime pointed out.

Add and fill shifts

When you add a shift or someone calls out, AI lists who is free and trained for it and drafts the text offering it.

How the training works.

We use a real opening or next week's real schedule.

  1. Write down your rules

    What the job truly requires. Who can work which station, and how many people each shift needs.

  2. Set it up together

    We build the prompts and the steps on your own accounts, with the tools you already use where possible.

  3. Check it against your own judgment

    You compare what AI produced with what you would have done, and we correct it until you trust it.

  4. Run it yourself

    You and your managers use it for the next opening and the next schedule. The decisions stay with you.

What AI should not do in hiring.

It should not reject anyone, rank people on its own or make the final call. Software that screens people out can repeat old biases without anyone noticing, and the employer answers for the result.

So I teach three habits. Give AI the requirements in writing and have it show which ones each applicant meets. Keep details that should not influence the decision, such as photos, ages and home addresses, out of what it sees. And have a person read the summaries and decide.

What I do not do.

I do not recruit, interview, run payroll or give legal advice. I am not a staffing agency or an HR consultant. I teach you and your managers to do your own hiring and scheduling faster, with AI doing the reading and the first draft.

Examples.

Illustrative setups that show what is possible. They are not client results.

Forty applications for one line cook

An owner gives AI the job requirements and forty applications. It returns a short summary of each one and which requirements it meets. The owner reads the summaries and decides who to call.

Next week's schedule, drafted

A manager gives AI the team's availability, the time-off requests and the staff each shift needs. AI drafts the schedule and lists the conflicts. The manager fixes them and posts it.

A Saturday call-out

A cook calls out sick. The manager asks AI who is free, trained for the station and not headed into overtime, then approves a text offering the shift to those people.

Questions, answered.

Can AI screen resumes for me?

It can do the reading. AI summarizes each application against the requirements you wrote, so you compare forty short summaries and not forty resumes. I teach a setup where a person reads the summaries and makes every decision.

Will AI reject good candidates?

It can, if you let it reject anyone. I teach you not to. AI sorts and summarizes, and nobody is turned down by software. If a summary looks thin, you open the resume.

Is it legal to use AI in hiring?

Yes, with care. Anti-discrimination law applies to a hiring decision whether a person or software made it, and the employer is responsible either way. That is why a person should decide and why details that should not matter are kept out of what AI sees. I am not a lawyer, so ask yours if you are unsure.

Can AI build my staff schedule?

It can write the first draft from your team's availability, time-off requests and the staffing each shift needs, and point out conflicts and overtime. You review it and post it.

Does it work with the scheduling app I already use?

Often. Many scheduling and point-of-sale apps can export availability and hours, and some connect to AI tools directly. In the free consultation I look at what you use and tell you what is possible.

What about applicants' personal information?

Resumes are full of it. I teach you which AI plans do not train on what you upload, how to leave out what is not needed, and when a private AI setup is the better choice.

Do you do the hiring or the scheduling for us?

No. I am not a recruiter or a staffing agency. I teach you and your managers to do both with AI, on your own accounts. The hiring decisions and the schedule stay with you.
